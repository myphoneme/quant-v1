Term vs Health Insurance: a plain-language starter guide

First time buying insurance and not sure what covers what? This guide explains the two most common policy types in plain terms — it's educational, not a recommendation of a specific plan or sum insured.

Term Life InsuranceHealth Insurance
What it protectsYour family's income if you pass away during the policy termYour savings from large hospital and treatment bills
Who gets the payoutYour nominated beneficiary, as a lump sumYou (or the hospital directly, via cashless claims), for eligible medical expenses
Typical triggerDeath of the insured during the policy termHospitalisation, and sometimes day-care procedures or pre/post-hospitalisation costs
Do you get money back if nothing happens?No, for a pure term plan (unless you choose a return-of-premium variant)No, it's not a savings product — but no-claim bonus can increase your cover
Common add-onsCritical illness rider, accidental death benefitMaternity cover, OPD cover, restoration benefit

When people typically consider term insurance

  • You have dependents (spouse, children, parents) who rely on your income
  • You have a home loan or other debt that shouldn't fall on your family
  • You want a large amount of cover for a relatively low premium, without any savings component

When people typically consider health insurance

  • You don't have adequate employer-provided health cover, or it ends when you change jobs
  • You want cover for your family, including parents, who may not be covered elsewhere
  • Rising hospital costs are a bigger financial risk to you than the annual premium
This is general education, not personalised advice. The right sum insured, term length and add-ons depend on your income, dependents, existing cover, and health history. Insurance is the subject matter of solicitation — read the policy wording carefully before buying.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need both term and health insurance?

For most people, yes — they cover different risks. Term insurance protects your family's income if you're no longer around; health insurance protects your savings from a large medical bill while you're alive. They aren't substitutes for each other.

Can Phoneme Quant tell me exactly which plan and sum insured I need?

No. This guide is educational only. Phoneme Quant distributes insurance through NJ Insurance Brokers Private Limited (Certificate No. POSPN72044) but does not provide personalised advice on which specific plan or amount suits you — that's a conversation to have directly when comparing plans, informed by your own income, dependents and existing cover.

Does a health insurance claim affect my term insurance?

No, they're independent policies from a claims perspective, though insurers may ask about your overall health history when you apply for either.

What's not covered by these policies?

Every policy has specific exclusions (like pre-existing conditions during a waiting period, or certain causes of death for term plans) — always read the policy wording and exclusions before buying, not just the marketing summary.
